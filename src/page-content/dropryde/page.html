The Challenge

  • Dropryde wasn't trying to solve bookings.
  • It was trying to solve everything that happens after a booking is created.
  • Customers needed confidence that payments were protected and providers were legitimate.
  • Providers needed transparent payouts, predictable workflows, and visibility into their business performance.
  • Affiliates required referral tracking and commission reporting.
  • Administrators needed complete operational oversight across payments, disputes, subscriptions, and compliance.
  • The platform needed to scale without increasing manual operational workload.
  • The challenge was not marketplace creation.
  • It was marketplace governance.

What Had

to Be Solved

  • The project started with five non-negotiable requirements:
  • Create trust between customers and providers before transactions occur.
  • Govern payments, commissions, refunds, and payouts through transparent workflows.
  • Introduce provider compliance and verification before marketplace participation.
  • Centralize operational oversight across users, transactions, and disputes.
  • Build an architecture capable of supporting long-term marketplace expansion.

Product
Strategy Decisions

Escrow-Based Financial Infrastructure

Payments could not move directly between customers and providers.
Stripe Connect with wallet management and escrow flows became the foundation of every financial interaction.
This created transparency across:
  • Customer payments
  • Provider earnings
  • Refunds
  • Affiliate commissions
  • Affiliate commissions
  • Withdrawal requests

Compliance Before Participation

Service providers were required to complete verification before they could accept bookings.

The platform introduced:

  • Identity verification
  • License validation
  • Insurance document uploads
  • Asset verification
  • Expiry monitoring
  • Administrative approval workflows
Participation in the marketplace became permission-based rather than automatic.

Governance as a Core Product Layer

The admin platform was designed as operational infrastructure rather than a reporting dashboard.

Administrators gained control over:
  • User verification
  • Provider approvals
  • Subscription plans
  • Dispute resolution
  • Withdrawal approvals
  • Withdrawal approvals
  • Platform health monitoring
  • Withdrawal approvals

Multi-Role Architecture

Discovery revealed four independent operational groups:

Users can analyze:

  • Customer
  • Service Provider
  • Affiliate
  • Super Admin
Each received unique permissions, workflows, and interfaces.
